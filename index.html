What the business has already earned

The digital gaps should not obscure the business signals. AJ reported roughly 20 residential-remodel jobs in December 2025, a goal of 30–40 and a two-week backlog. In March 2026, he said a slow period had reversed and the company was “super busy” with more work than it could handle. In September 2026, he reported approximately 15 jobs per week and said at least some fabrication is handled internally depending on customer needs. These are management statements from different dates and may use different job definitions; they are evidence of operating activity, not a revenue trend. [1] [2] [3]

The commercial issue is infrastructure—not proof that the trade business is fake

Stone By AJ has a consistent phone number, physical trade category, Facebook project content, a claimed Yelp page, public directory history and a Google Maps profile with reviews. That supports a legitimate operating business. The weakness is that demand and proof live on rented platforms. A prospect can discover the company, but there is no controlled destination that explains the process, qualifies the project, captures photos and dimensions, sets response expectations or records marketing source.

Strongest current asset

December meeting notes say most leads came through referrals, including reciprocal relationships with designers and remodelers. Public review themes reinforce responsiveness, workmanship, value and repeat business.

The competitive comparison buyers are making

Nearby competitors are not necessarily better craftsmen, but their digital presentation makes them easier to evaluate. Their sites explain materials, process, timelines, showrooms, financing, warranties, galleries and estimate steps before a homeowner calls. Stone By AJ’s advantage should be direct accountability and credible local proof; its disadvantage is that those advantages are not organized into a buyer journey.

The original sales conversation: legitimate operator, weak immediate buyer readiness

The December meeting does not sound like an agency owner collecting tactics. It sounds like a real trades operator with low marketing maturity and limited urgency. AJ reported being booked two weeks out, relying primarily on referrals and spending about $100 per month on Facebook without knowing its bottom-line effect. When demand recovered in March, he said the company had more work than it could handle and deferred the conversation. That combination explains a short discovery call: there was a real business, but no validated capacity, attribution or investment case for scaling ads.

Recommended engagement structure: begin with a paid foundation project—not open-ended free consulting and not a media retainer. The deliverable should cover domain/site launch, Google profile governance, identity cleanup, qualified intake, measurement and a referral/review system. Paid demand generation should be a later decision gate.
